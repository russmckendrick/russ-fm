01Overview & goals

russ.fm is a personal vinyl archive. The previous site leaned on the visual conventions of streaming apps — dark chrome, pop covers, large hero images — which felt at odds with the physicality of the collection it documents. This redesign shifts the metaphor away from the streaming player and toward the record shop: thin rules, numbered sections, a catalogue-style density, and copy that reads like the stock card stapled to a bin divider.

The site should feel like walking into a well-kept second-hand shop — considered, over-labelled, quietly obsessive — not like opening another streaming app.

Primary goals

  • Reinforce the archive metaphor. Treat each record as an accessioned object with a catalogue number, date of addition, format, label, and country — surfaced as metadata, not decoration.
  • Let the covers do the colouring. Every view can inherit a dominant colour from the record on screen, tinting hero washes, shadows, and swatches. The page becomes an extension of the sleeve you're looking at.
  • Make the collection browsable at speed. Dense walls, drag-scrolling, grid/list toggles, letter filters, and pagination — all designed for someone who actually has 3,000+ records to dig through.
  • Be genuinely tweakable. Density, mono labels, cover-tint intensity, and theme are all first-class knobs, because taste about density is personal.

02Audience & context

Who uses it

  • The collector (primary). Russ himself — scanning recent additions, resolving what to spin, remembering what's in the catalogue, occasionally pulling a random pick when indecisive.
  • Friends & fellow diggers (secondary). Skimming for taste overlap, arguing about pressings, discovering artists through the lens of someone with genuine taste.
  • Passers-through (tertiary). People who landed via a share link and will stay ninety seconds — should still be instantly legible, no onboarding.

Use cases informing the design

  • "What did I just buy?" → Recent additions wall on home.
  • "I don't know what to listen to" → Random page, one keystroke away.
  • "Do I own anything by X?" → Artist browse + search.
  • "How has my collection shifted?" → Stats page with decade bars, genre donut, additions-over-time.
  • "What did I play this year?" → Wrapped (year-in-review).

03Design principles

P · 01

Metadata is the aesthetic

Catalogue numbers, dates, formats, and labels are not chrome — they are the typography. Mono labels do the heavy lifting; illustration does not.

P · 02

The cover is the canvas

Every surface that can pull colour from the record in frame, does. The system loses most of its soul without this.

P · 03

Dense by default

Walls scroll, tiles are small, lists are tight. A sparse toggle exists for those who want it, but the default says "this person owns a lot of records."

P · 04

One family, many weights

A single grotesk (Inter Tight) handles every display and body need. Mono (JetBrains Mono) is reserved for anything that behaves like a label or a catalogue number.

P · 05

Thin rules, sharp corners

No rounded cards, no drop shadows except where the sleeve is literally being rendered as an object. Borders are hairlines on paper.

P · 06

Tweaks are first-class

Density, mono labels, cover-colour on/off, tint intensity, and theme are exposed in-page. The design should not hide its own levers.

04Voice & aesthetic

Voice

Clipped, cataloguing, never clever. Headings lean slightly formal ("Section 02 · Catalogue"). Metadata uses abbreviations a record shop would use: LP, 2LP, 12", CAT. A01, 12 APR 26. Avoid hype language ("discover", "explore", "journey"), avoid emoji, avoid exclamation points.

Aesthetic references

  • Divider cards and hand-stamped bin labels from independent record shops.
  • Classical-music box-set typography — Deutsche Grammophon, ECM catalogue sheets.
  • Library science: accession numbers, date-of-addition, provenance fields.
  • Print catalogues from design presses (Unit Editions, Display, Spector).

What it is not

  • Not Spotify. Not Apple Music. No now-playing bar, no waveforms, no mood gradients.
  • Not a blog. Long-form prose is possible but not the frame.
  • Not a Discogs clone. Discogs is transactional; this is curatorial.

06Colour system

Two themes (paper and ink) share the same semantic tokens. Neither is a pure grey — both lean warm, because grey-on-grey is what streaming apps do and this one shouldn't. A single accent (--hl, crimson) is used sparingly for active states and highlights; everything else is drawn from the record currently on screen.

Cover-colour rules

  • Every album record ships with a dom (dominant) and accent colour, extracted from its sleeve.
  • Hero washes use the dominant colour at --tint-strength (default 0.85) with mix-blend-mode: multiply on light theme, screen at 0.55× on dark.
  • Cover shadows use --shadow set from dom, giving each sleeve a subtle tinted float.
  • The Random page re-tints the entire viewport on every shuffle — the single most satisfying moment in the design.

07Spacing & tokens

Spatial rhythm

All spacing sits on a 4px grid. The canonical vertical rhythm between sections is 56px; inside a card or row it drops to 18–24px. Horizontal page padding is 64px on desktop, 32px below 1200px, 22px on mobile.

08Components

A small kit of components is reused across every page. If a need doesn't fit one of these, the instinct should be "remix, don't invent."

Core

  • Top bar. Brand mark + catalogue tag · nav · search · theme toggle. Sticky, single rule below.
  • Hero. Split layout — cover on one side, big title + chip-row metadata on the other. Tinted wash behind, sharp corners, optional carousel dots.
  • Tile. Square cover + title + artist + year. Hover reveals a mono metadata strip sliding up from the bottom edge. Optional cat-number badge top-left.
  • Artist tile. Circular photo, tinted shadow, ranked number, release count + country.
  • Catalogue row. 7-column grid mimicking a shop bin divider — number, title w/ swatch, artist, year, format, genre, added date.
  • Chip. Sharp-cornered pill with hairline border; mono or grotesk variant; button variant has hover / is-on states.
  • Key-value block. Grid with 1px gaps showing through, used for release specs, stats totals, artist quick-facts.
  • Pager. Summary + prev · numbered pages with ellipsis · next, mono throughout.
  • Sidebar aside. Aggregated facts (totals, histogram, scrobbles, genres). Sticky on desktop, inline on mobile.
  • Tweaks panel. Fixed bottom-right surface, surfaces when the toolbar toggle is flipped.

Feature blocks

  • Decade bars / top-years bars — plain coloured rectangles, no gridlines.
  • Donut — hand-drawn SVG segments with paper strokes between slices.
  • Additions-over-time histogram — 1-px-gap columns, one per month, year labels at every January.
  • Golden-year / most-active-month — single enormous number treatments.
  • Tracklist — two-column table with row-number, title, duration, play handle.

09Page architecture

Home

Carousel hero of recent featured pressings → recent additions wall (drag-scroll) → random picks (tinted cards) → top artists strip → sidebar with totals, decade histogram, recent scrobbles, and genre cloud.

Album detail

Full-bleed tinted hero with the sleeve as a shadowed object on one side and the title + artist + chip-row on the other. Body splits into a tracklist + "more from this artist" main column, and a sidebar with release details, identifiers (Discogs, barcode), and external links.

Artist detail

Tinted hero with circular portrait and large name; 4-stat block; bio prose; full catalogue table of releases; related artists; sidebar with quick facts + externals.

Albums / Artists browse

Kicker + title + counts header. Filters: sort / view toggle / genre chip-row (albums) or A–Z letter row (artists). Grid or list. Pager at the bottom.

Stats

Hero with a stacked-sleeves vignette + totals pills. Then: 4 KPI cards, decade bars, genre donut, golden year, top-5 years, top-8 artists (with swatch strips), artist depth, most-active month, recents, additions-over-time, from-the-crates, random artists.

Random

One huge sleeve, its title, an artist link, and a Shuffle button (keyboard: Space). The entire viewport re-tints on every shuffle. A peek strip below shows a handful of next possibilities.

Wrapped

Year-in-review with a giant year-word treatment, totals, Album of the Year block, ranked top-10 with play bars, and new-discoveries artists.

10Interaction patterns

  • Drag-scroll walls. Horizontal tile strips scroll with momentum; prev/next buttons appear on hover of the wrap.
  • Hover reveal. Tiles expose format / tracks / year on hover, sliding in from the bottom edge without displacing the sleeve.
  • Hash routing. Every view owns a URL (#/album/a01, #/artist/throbbing-gristle, #/stats) — refresh keeps state, links are shareable.
  • Keyboard. / focuses search. Space shuffles on the Random page.
  • Pagination. Page changes scroll smoothly to top; filter/sort/view toggles reset to page 1.
  • Tint propagation. Changing the featured album (carousel, random) retargets every var(--tint) across the page in a 600–700ms ease.
  • Tweaks. All changes apply live and persist to localStorage. Cover-colour off, sparse density, and mono-labels off all give legitimately different experiences.

11Accessibility

  • Contrast. Body copy (--ink-2) on paper meets WCAG AA at body size; mono-labels (--ink-dim) are used only at <12px decorative level and always have a visible sibling element conveying the same information.
  • Target size. Every interactive element meets 36×36 minimum; pager buttons, chips, and tweaks controls are sized accordingly.
  • Focus. Default focus ring inherited; no outline: none anywhere.
  • Motion. Transitions cap at 700ms; no looping or parallax. The Random page's shuffle animation can be skipped by starting any keyboard action.
  • Semantics. Proper landmarks (<header>, <main>, <nav>, <footer>), lists are lists, buttons are buttons.
  • Dark mode. Theme class sits on <html> so tokens cascade properly through body; blend modes switch from multiply to screen so tint washes read on the ink palette.

12Open questions & roadmap

Known gaps

  • Placeholder SVG sleeves stand in for real cover art; production will pull from Discogs.
  • Artist photos are placeholders; licensing and a fallback strategy needs a decision.
  • Search currently renders as chrome; actual search implementation (client-side index? server?) is open.
  • The Wrapped page is sketched for 2025 only; the yearly regeneration mechanic needs defining.

Candidate next moves

  • Real data binding. Wire the redesign to the existing russ.fm data source.
  • Per-record typography. Optional override letting a single album specify its own display face for maximum sleeve fidelity.
  • Printable catalogue. A print stylesheet that produces a folio-style PDF of the entire collection.
  • Mobile-first pass. Audit every page below 720px; the frame is responsive but the interaction details (hover-reveal, drag-scroll) need touch equivalents.
  • Motion grammar. Codify transition durations & easings into tokens (--ease-in, --dur-sm) so new components inherit consistent motion.
